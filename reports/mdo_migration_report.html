Verification report · hpraptor

RAPTOR MDO Migration

The framework now runs a real gradient-based optimization over the discipline modules, with propulsion-architecture choice as a continuous design variable. Getting there first required fixing a defect that had been setting the architecture comparison's headline result.

tests 169 passing openmdao 3.44.0 dymos 1.15.1 casadi 3.7.2

What changed

Two things, in this order — the second was not trustworthy until the first was done.

A units-scale defect in the fuel-flow model was inflating fuel burn by 47–400× for the three ICE-based architectures, and only those three. It had determined the outcome of every architecture comparison run so far.

The OpenMDAO package was rebuilt from a disconnected stub into a coupled model that wraps the actual m2–m5 modules, with design variables, constraints, and a driver that runs. The continuous architecture relaxation is now a live design variable inside it.

The whole mission moved to dymos — all five phases, not just cruise. Each migration step exposed defects the static model could not: the trajectory optimizer searches control profiles, and it finds every place where energy is not conserved.

The fuel cell got its real physics back. The polarization curve is now solved differentiably instead of being replaced by a constant.

What the trajectory optimizer found

Three defects, each invisible to a single-point evaluation.

A static model evaluates one operating condition with the power split hemmed in by other terms. An optimal-control solver chooses a whole control profile, and it will find any path where the physics lets it get something for nothing. It found three.

1 — all-electric created energy

Every architecture splits shaft demand into an electric and a fuel share. All-electric has no fuel path, so its fuel share was delivered and never charged for. At k_electric = 0 the aircraft flew on nothing at all. Present in the CasADi original too. The battery now carries the entire demand regardless of the split.

2 — control overshoot between collocation nodes

dymos enforces control bounds at the discretization nodes, not between them. k_electric overshot past 1.0 in the interior, making the fuel share negative, which reversed the sign of the fuel-flow gate: the vehicle gained mass and recharged its battery to full during the back transition, and total mission energy came out negative. The split is now clamped inside the physics, so no caller and no discretization can reach it.

3 — a state that meant two different things

This one was mine. All five phases were linked on v — but v is the vertical rate in the VTOL phases and airspeed in the wingborne ones. Linking them asserts "climb rate at the end of hover equals airspeed at the start of transition", which is physically meaningless and over-constrained the problem into infeasibility: airspeed reached −26 m/s. Now only quantities that mean the same thing on both sides of a boundary are linked.

And a guard so this cannot pass silently again

One run printed "converged" while the driver's own success flag was False. A collocation solver that stops at an infeasible point still returns a complete, plausible looking trajectory. Every solve is now audited afterwards against physics that cannot be negotiated — state of charge may not rise, mass may not rise, airspeed may not go negative — and a violating result is labelled PHYSICALLY INCONSISTENT — do not use these numbers regardless of what the driver reports.

Fuel-cell polarization, differentiably

The bisection was the right physics; it just could not be differentiated.

FuelCellParams.system_efficiency inverts the polarization curve with 30 bisection steps. Correct, but bisection branches on comparisons, so neither complex step nor algorithmic differentiation can follow it — which is why the differentiable paths substituted a flat eta = 0.55.

Two routes share one residual: a fixed-count unrolled Newton solve for use inside the ODE, where a nested component cannot go, and FuelCellPolarizationComp, an ImplicitComponent whose Newton solver supplies derivatives through the implicit function theorem — the idiomatic OpenMDAO formulation, and the reference the unrolled version is checked against. Agreement is 1.4×10−4.

Two things surfaced while building it. Net power is not monotonic in current density — it peaks, falls, then rises again once the 0.3 V practical floor clamps the voltage, so the solve is confined to the first rising branch and saturates above the deliverable maximum rather than diverging onto the falling one. And the default FuelCellParams is internally inconsistent: it declares P_max = 8000 W while its own 60-cell, 200 cm² stack peaks at 2373 W — and its mass is sized from the declared figure.

The fuel-flow defect

Found while establishing a baseline to compare the ported model against.

Every ICE architecture ended its fuel calculation by smoothing a maximum against a floor:

fuel_flow = switch * _smooth_max_ca(fuel_flow, ice._a * 0.5) _smooth_max_ca(a, b, eps) = 0.5 * (a + b + sqrt((a-b)² + eps)) eps = 0.01

The approximation error of that smoothing is √eps / 2, so an absolute eps = 0.01 injects a floor of 0.05 into whatever it touches. That is invisible on shaft power (order 10⁴ W) and fatal on fuel mass flow (order 10⁻⁴ kg/s).

The guard was also unreachable

It mirrored numpy's max(m_dot, _a*0.5). But with m_dot = _a + _b·P and _b, P ≥ 0, we always have m_dot ≥ _a > 0.5·_a — the condition can never bind. The code was paying a 400× error to smooth a branch that never fires. It was deleted outright, not retuned.

At mission scale: 500 s of ICE operation should burn 0.063 kg. The model burned 25 kg — from a 5 kg tank.

Only series, parallel, and series_parallel called that line. The other three architectures compute fuel flow by different formulas and were untouched — which is exactly the split the stored sweep showed.

Why this matters for the paper

Before the fix the ICE architectures sat 200–400× above everything else, so the relaxation selected all_electric at every weight class — an artifact, not a physical finding. They now land in the same band as the others, and the architecture comparison is genuinely competitive for the first time.

The smoothing helpers now take a scale argument (eps = (1e-3·scale)²) so the error stays ~0.05% of the operands whatever their units. The dimensionless call sites — motor load_frac, eta, turbine x_ratio — were carrying the same 0.05 absolute error, i.e. 5–50%, and are corrected by the same change.

What OpenMDAO replaced

The previous package was not doing what its docstring said.

It contained five components with their own toy physics — flat efficiencies, a hardcoded C_D0 — that imported nothing from m2–m5. And its optimizer never ran: design variables were declared as IndepVarComp outputs, add_design_var / add_objective / add_constraint were never called, and the runner invoked run_model() rather than run_driver(). It solved the coupled system once at whatever values you passed in.

The rebuilt package is ~1 290 lines across eight components, each wrapping the real discipline module. The m_tow feedback edge makes it a genuinely coupled MDA, converged by NonlinearBlockGS with Aitken relaxation — the same role the hand-rolled 5-iteration loop plays in initial_sizing.py, except derivatives across the converged loop now exist.

The relaxation validates

Starting from a uniform 1/6 blend, the discreteness penalty λ Σ wi(1−wi) drove the weights to 0.9991 on a single architecture (residual penalty 0.0018), and it selected the same architecture the brute-force sweep found best — in one gradient-based solve, without enumerating the six.

That said, every geometric variable finished pinned at a bound (AR at 14, wing_loading at 300, disk_loading at 100, t_spar at 1 mm) and MTOW settled at 9.58 kg against a 5 kg payload — a 52% payload fraction, high for this vehicle class. The optimizer is behaving correctly; the bounds are doing work the physics should be doing. That is a model-realism gap, listed below.

The energy-conservation hole

Found by the trajectory optimizer within one solve of the cruise phase being built.

In casadi_arch_power_split and its numpy twin, every architecture splits the shaft demand into an electric and a fuel share:

P_elec_mech = P_demand * k_electric P_fuel_mech = P_demand * (1 - k_electric) if arch == "all_electric": pass # P_fuel_mech is computed, then never served

All-electric has no fuel path, so that fuel share was simply never supplied — and never charged for. The vehicle delivered the full shaft power while drawing only k_electric of it from the battery.

The static MDO never exposed this because it evaluates cruise at one condition with k_electric hemmed in by other terms. Give an optimal-control solver a free control profile and it finds the hole immediately: the first converged cruise run settled on k_electric = 0.314 with zero fuel burned, and reported an energy figure roughly 2.4× too low.

Fixed in both implementations

All-electric now routes the entire shaft demand through the battery regardless of k_electric. The other five architectures were checked and are sound — each has a real fuel path that accounts for its share. Both the CasADi and numpy versions were corrected together so the twin stays faithful, and a parametrized regression test asserts battery draw never falls below shaft power delivered.

Cruise phase in dymos

One segment, integrated — not a point evaluation.

States are range, mass, and state of charge; controls are airspeed and power split; the architecture relaxation enters as a time-invariant trajectory parameter. Because the segment is integrated, mass falls as fuel burns and the required lift coefficient drifts with it — coupling a single design point cannot represent.

The symmetric start does not work

A single solve from z = 0 hit the iteration limit with weights stuck at 0.196 each, barely moved from the uniform 1/6. That is the saddle point predicted earlier: at z = 0 the discreteness penalty's gradient is exactly zero by symmetry, so the term meant to force a buildable architecture gives no first-order signal at the only natural starting point.

Multi-start from each architecture in turn resolves it — the same remedy the CasADi sweep already uses via warm_start_arch_idx, which keeps the two pipelines comparable.

The five-phase mission

All phases in dymos, with the architecture shared across the whole trajectory.

VTOL climb → forward transition → cruise → back transition → VTOL landing

dymos owns the transcription, the phase linkage and the derivatives across the whole trajectory; what remains in mission_problem.py is the mission definition — durations, bounds, boundary conditions, objective. The resulting problem, read back from the model:

Module status

Where each module stands for the per-module review.

m1_mission

DEM, wind, path building not in MDO

Correctly a source of fixed data rather than a discipline component — terrain and wind are parameters, not design variables.

Open: terrain clearance is not yet a constraint in the MDO model. It needs trajectory position to constrain against, which arrives with the trajectory phase.

m2_geometry

wing, fuselage, rotor, tail wired

GeometryComp calls the real WingPlanform, estimate_fuselage_geometry, size_rotors_from_disk_loading and size_tail_from_wing — no reimplementation.

The AeroSandbox 3D/VLM layer stays outside the optimizer by design: a full VLM stability solve costs ~1 s and cannot sit inside a mass-closure loop. It remains a post-hoc verification and visualization step.

m3_structures

spar sizing, stress margin wired reformulated

StructuresComp exposes t_spar_mm as a design variable with g4 as a constraint, replacing the internal bisection search (minimum_feasible_t_spar_mm). A root-find nested inside a component is non-smooth and its derivative is meaningless; letting the outer optimizer drive the thickness gives the same answer with real gradients and visible constraint activity.

Finding: g4 settles at −0.70 — not binding. The spar is gauge-limited at the 1 mm lower bound, not stress-limited, confirming the earlier module audit. Worth revisiting with CFRP or heavier MTOW classes.

m4_aero

parasite drag, drag polar wired

AeroComp runs the component skin-friction buildup against real wetted areas, so wing area genuinely feeds back into required power. A regression test asserts C_D0 moves when wing loading does.

flat_plate_cf needed a complex-step fix: max() raises on complex operands, so the Re floor is now applied on the real part.

m5_propulsion

power required, architecture relaxation wired defect fixed

The centerpiece. architecture_np.py is a complex-step-safe numpy twin of the CasADi blending, verified against the original to 3×10⁻⁶ on values and 2.6×10⁻⁵ on gradients across six architectures and six operating points.

The fuel-cell constant is gone: the numpy path now solves the real polarization curve differentiably (fuel_cell_polarization.py), matching the bisection reference to 1.4e-4. It is the one architecture that is deliberately NO LONGER a transliteration of the CasADi path, so it is verified against the physics rather than against CasADi.

m6_dynamics

3-DoF equations of motion migrated

Holds the full 3-DoF equations — range, altitude, airspeed, mass, SOC — with phase-dependent branches for VTOL climb, transition, cruise and landing.

Rewritten as eom_np.py, complex-step safe and vectorized for dymos. Both hardcoded Quito constants are gone: path_heading_deg (was pinned to due north, so the headwind projection was only ever right for a north-south route) and ground elevation (was h - 2850) are now arguments. Phases are separate functions, so dymos resolves the branch once at setup rather than at every node.

m7_trajectory

optimal control, collocation all 5 phases migrated convergence tuning

All five phases now run in dymos (Gauss-Lobatto) under hpraptor_mdao/trajectory/: 361 design variables, 72 inequality and 223 equality constraints, with the architecture relaxation as a single trajectory-level variable shared across every phase.

The trajectory is physically consistent — state of charge falls monotonically through all five phases and no speed goes negative — but the solve still reaches the iteration limit rather than a clean KKT point. The CasADi OCP is kept until that is settled, since it is the only independent reference to check against.

m8_optimizer

sweeps and benchmarks unified entry point

All three studies now run from one place — python -m hpraptor_mdao.run {sizing|cruise|mission} — each printing its formulation before solving and able to emit its N2 diagram. The legacy CasADi MTOW sweep in m8_optimizer/sizing_opt.py still exists alongside it as the validation reference.

Complex-step groundwork

Three source-level fixes were needed before OpenMDAO could differentiate the existing modules.

Complex step puts the perturbation in the imaginary part, so any comparison must be taken on the real part only — a bare comparison either raises on complex operands or branches on the perturbation and destroys the derivative. All three fixes are additive: real-input behavior is unchanged.

  • atmosphere.isa_temperature — max() on the 200 K floor
  • parasite_drag.flat_plate_cf — max() on the Reynolds floor
  • spar_sizing.size_spar — the I_xx > 0 guard and the feasibility flag

A fourth surfaced only under complex arithmetic: np.tanh overflows for arguments past ~350, where real tanh simply saturates to 1.0. The switch function now clamps its argument at ±30, where tanh is saturated to within 2×10⁻²⁶ and the discarded derivative is zero to machine precision.

A verification trap worth knowing

Once design variables are declared, OpenMDAO relevance-prunes the partials it computes: derivatives not on a design-variable-to-response path are reported as exactly zero. Against finite difference this looks like a catastrophic 100% error on perfectly correct code. Partials must be checked on a model without design variables declared; check_totals covers the rest.

With that accounted for, all 115 non-trivial partial pairs agree with finite difference. Three apparent failures were traced to finite difference, not complex step: two involve fuel_lhv (order 4×10⁷, where the default FD step is below double-precision resolution) and one is the discreteness penalty at z = 0, whose gradient is exactly zero by symmetry.

Open items

Known and unresolved, in rough order of how much they affect conclusions.

  1. The five-phase solve does not converge cleanly yet The trajectory is physically consistent — monotone state of charge, no negative speeds — but SLSQP reaches its iteration limit instead of a KKT point, and two runs of the identical setup disagreed on success. Scaling and initial guesses need work before the numbers are quotable.
  2. Trajectory and sizing are not yet coupled The dymos phases take S_ref, AR, C_D0 and A_rotor as fixed parameters rather than from the sizing model. Closing that loop is what makes this one framework rather than two that happen to agree.
  3. AeroSandbox cannot be complex-stepped arctan2 has no complex loop and raises outright — the library is built for CasADi AD. Geometry construction is cheap enough to sit in the loop (0.42 ms) but VLM is not (329 ms, ~145 s per optimization, with finite-difference gradients). VLM accuracy should reach the loop through the surrogate fit that m4_aero/surrogate_aero.py already builds.
  4. FuelCellParams is internally inconsistent It declares P_max = 8000 W and sizes its mass from that, while its own 60-cell 200 cm² stack peaks at 2373 W.
  5. Fuel-cell efficiency mismatch Hardcoded 0.55 in both differentiable paths vs ~0.40 from the polarization curve, biasing architecture selection toward the fuel cell by roughly 37%.
  6. Design variables pinned at bounds All four geometric variables finish on a bound. The model lacks the constraints that would otherwise stop it — rotor/airframe packaging, structural limits that actually bind, a realistic empty-weight buildup.
  7. Convergence regressions in the CasADi sweep The 100 and 200 kg classes now fail for several architectures that previously converged. Different physics gives a different landscape; the 200 kg case is largely failing and needs warm-start work.
  8. Penalty gradient vanishes at the symmetric start At z = 0 all weights are 1/6 and the discreteness penalty's gradient is exactly zero, so it cannot break the tie on its own — the energy term has to. A saddle point worth warm-starting away from.
  9. Two optimizer entry points m8_optimizer/sizing_opt.py drives CasADi; hpraptor_mdao/problem.py drives OpenMDAO. Unifying them depends on item one.
